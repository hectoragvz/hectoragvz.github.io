Pufferosaurus

(back home)

A tiny recurrent policy learned to avoid cacti and meteors. The model, game simulation, and rendering all run locally in the browser.

Everything above is C compiled to WebAssembly, including the trained policy and its recurrent state.

Passing a cactus and a meteor earns a reward, and both jumping and colliding have associated penalties. Ducking when not needed was also penalized as the dinosaur learned that a ducking hitbox is ultimataely smaller and more optimal as it collides less often -obviously, but I did not want the dinosaur to be in that position all the time (boring). The jump penalty discourages repeated jumping without prescribing when a jump should happen; this separated the demo from just a dinosaur which always stood still or jumped all the time. I attach some of my dev notes for this project below.

Training ran for 10 million environment steps with PufferLib; the browser loads the resulting checkpoint directly.

A huge shoutout to the PufferLib team for making such an awesome resource. Go and check their website 🐡.

Notes

August 8, 2026

Today I turned the Dino evaluation into a fixed 100-seed test suite. The policy now has to survive the full 2,000-step limit at 1x and 2× speed transitions, and the seven-pass curriculum. It also has to handle low jump-only meteors, flexible meteors, and high duck-only meteors without jumping from the ground.

The first 100M-step candidate learned the duck decision, but it was still only at fixed 1×. I then tested two ways of mixing fixed-1× episodes with the original seven-pass 1×-to-2× curriculum:

Training mix 1× 2× Up Down Multi Curric.
Original curriculum 64 100 100 64 64 89
50% fixed 1× 85 85 85 85 85 86
25% fixed 1× 71 100 100 71 71 90
Targeted fixed 1× meteors 85 100 100 85 85 95

Episodes reaching the 2,000-step limit out of 100. Higher is better; a candidate qualifies only at 100 in every suite.

The 50/50 run improved fixed 1× but cost the policy its strong 2× behavior. The 25/75 run restored 2× and transition behavior while keeping a smaller fixed-1× gain. The remaining failures are mostly low, jump-only meteors at fixed 1×, while duck-only meteor behavior is still correct.

A targeted fixed-1× curriculum kept the 25/75 mix, used a 50% meteor rate throughout fixed-1× episodes, and sampled 75% of those meteors from the jump-only height band. It matched the best fixed-1× result while restoring perfect 2× and upward transition results. It is the best candidate so far and now qualifies across every fixed suite. The final evaluator change excludes a meteor first encountered near the 2,000-step limit when the episode ends before it can be passed, rather than marking an otherwise successful episode as a meteor failure.

The final evaluation passed all six suites. Every one of the 700 fixed-seed episodes reached the 2,000-step limit, there were no first-obstacle crashes, every completed meteor-band encounter passed, and the Dino made no ground-level jumps for duck-only meteors. That makes this the first checkpoint ready for making it to the browser demo. These are some of the testing points I focused the demos on.

The newest demo test also makes a useful policy choice obvious: the Dino ducks almost constantly when it is not jumping. Ducking reduces the ground-level hitbox, and the current reward gives it no cost, so a recurrent policy can safely treat ducking as its default posture. The next isolated experiment will add a very small per-frame ground-duck penalty. A needless long duck should then become expensive, while a short duck beneath a high meteor remains much cheaper than a collision. The fixed evaluator will stay unchanged to verify that the Dino still ducks correctly when it must.

August 4, 2026

After testing the 2× speed toggle approach, I realized it was not working well at all: it seemed that learning stopped and neither 1x spped and 2x speed returned successful results at random speed changes. I ended up going with a simpler progression instead: every episode begins at 1×, then the game automatically and permanently switches to 2× after the dinosaur passes seven obstacles (which is honestly what the real Chrome game implements). The policy was trained and tested for this exact transition, and it is now running here on the site. I prioritized working on other parts of the project I still want to include, and now think of adding a label for each new "difficulty" that is added live to the game (currently score and speed only).

August 3, 2026

Currently working to make the "increase by 2x speed" button work here in the browser. The first six-observation candidate runs, but its 1× speed performance is just bad. Across 100 normal-speed episodes it averaged 2.34 passes and 377 steps, crashed before the first obstacle 28 times, and never reached the 2,000-step limit. The 5-observation policy (which only had 1x speed training) averaged 17 passes and reached the limit in all 100 episodes, such an obvious difference.
